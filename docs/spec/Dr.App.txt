## 0. Revision Log

### 0.1 What changed in v2.2 and why

Version 2.2 is the first revision to change the **commercial** case rather than the technical one, and it does so because an external fact changed underneath the document. In April 2026 the National Health Authority cut the Digital Health Incentive Scheme rate for outpatient records from ₹20 to ₹5 and restricted eligibility. The return-on-investment argument that v1.0 through v2.1 placed at the centre of the product was overstated by roughly a factor of five. Risk R6 — written in v2.0 to warn against exactly this dependency — had already materialised five months before it was recorded.

The remaining changes follow from a market analysis that re-ranked the launch verticals, and from a review of the tenancy implementation that found one latent defect severe enough to gate the first server endpoint.

## 1. Overview & Executive Summary

### 1.1 Executive Thesis

PractixOS is a verticalized Practice Operating System designed specifically for independent outpatient medical practices in India. It launches in a single clinical vertical — **Dentistry** — on an architecture in which the operational differences between specialties are expressed as per-tenant configuration rather than as code, so that Ophthalmology, Dermatology, Paediatrics and Mental Health Counselling follow without a rebuild, and general outpatient polyclinics after them. *(v2.2: v1.0–v2.1 launched two verticals simultaneously; see §2.4 for why that changed.)*

In the Indian private healthcare ecosystem, generic appointment schedulers fail because they address scheduling as a standalone administrative task. In reality, clinic viability is determined by specialty-specific workflows:

* Dental clinics are capital-intensive operations constrained by physical operatory chairs, external dental fabrication laboratories, and visiting specialists.
* Mental health practices are time-bounded operations governed by strict clinical confidentiality, recurring appointments, and high unrecoverable losses from patient no-shows.
* Across all Indian private practices, front-desk staff have limited digital literacy, digital personal data regulations are tightening under the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023, and patient footfall spans diverse socioeconomic and linguistic backgrounds.

PractixOS rejects the marketplace aggregator model, which commoditizes doctors, and the horizontal calendar model, which creates administrative burden. Instead it provides an end-to-end operational operating system that runs friction-free on low-cost hardware, embeds natively into WhatsApp, supports major Indic languages (English, Marathi, Telugu, Bengali, Konkani), enforces medical privacy law, and pays for itself out of revenue the clinic is currently losing to no-shows, abandoned treatment plans and unbilled work (Section 3.3).

### 1.2 Core Product Pillars

* **Zero-App Patient Engagement.** Patients interact entirely through sovereign clinic web portals (Progressive Web Apps) and two-way WhatsApp Business flows, eliminating app-download drop-off.
* **Bifurcated Localization Model.** High-velocity dense interfaces for clinicians and staff, paired with generous native-script Indic typography and vernacular voice notes for patients. This is a typographic and interaction-design commitment, not only a translation commitment — see Section 6.
* **Specialty-Specific Operational Workflows.** Interactive 32-tooth graphical odontograms and lab trackers for dentists; PIN-encrypted psychotherapy vaults for counsellors.
* **Measured Revenue Recovery.** *(Promoted to a pillar in v2.2.)* The platform does not assert a return on investment; it records one. Every no-show prevented by a confirmed reminder, every booking secured by a deposit, every dormant treatment plan resumed by a recall and every lab remake avoided is written to an auditable ledger with its evidence reference (FR-7.4). The owner sees what the software recovered, in rupees, from their own data.
* **Regulatory Compliance.** Native Milestone 1/2/3 integration with the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission. This is primarily a compliance and interoperability capability; the Digital Health Incentive Scheme adds a modest secondary payment on top, not a subsidy that funds the subscription (see Section 3.4).
* **Autonomous Schedule & Cash-Flow Protection.** Two-way synchronization with personal Google and Outlook calendars, dynamic UPI reservation deposits, and rule-based follow-up fee calculation.
* **Structural Cost Discipline.** *(New in v2.0.)* The platform runs entirely on free and open-source software with no commercial licence anywhere in the production path, on infrastructure that costs ₹0 per month at pilot scale. Low cost is an architectural property, not a discount.

## 2. Why This App & The Problems It Solves

### 2.1 The Operational Realities of Indian Private Practice

Independent clinics in India operate in high-pressure environments that render Western practice management software unusable:

* **High OPD velocity.** Solo doctors routinely consult 30 to 80 patients within condensed three- to four-hour morning or evening windows. Administrative actions taking longer than 15 seconds per patient are abandoned for paper registers.
* **Front-desk attrition and low digital proficiency.** Receptionists often possess basic technical skills and turn over frequently. Systems with nested menus or English-only clinical jargon create data-entry bottlenecks and scheduling mistakes.
* **Severe revenue leakage from no-shows.** Urban outpatient cancellation and no-show rates routinely exceed 20% to 30%. For a counsellor holding a 50-minute block or a dentist reserving an operatory chair for an endodontic procedure, an unnotified absence produces zero chair utilisation and an unrecoverable loss.
* **Linguistic diversity.** Patients span a wide socioeconomic range, from affluent non-English speakers who prefer local languages to lower-income workers who rely on voice notes. Forcing English-only communication leads to missed appointments, medication confusion, and disputes over consultation fees.

### 2.2 The Failures of Incumbent Solutions

* **Directory aggregators (e.g. Practo Ray).** Aggregators run consumer search engines that divert a clinic's existing patient base to competing sponsored listings. Doctors pay high monthly subscriptions only to have their brand diluted and their patient communication co-opted.
* **Prescription-only pads (e.g. HealthPlix, Eka Care).** Effective for generating quick digital prescriptions, but they do not manage front-desk queues, physical chair constraints, visiting consultant fee splits, or external fabrication labs.
* **Legacy vertical software (e.g. Dentee).** Built on desktop-era architectures, requiring extensive training, lacking real-time WhatsApp automation, and running poorly on modern mobile devices and reception tablets.

### 2.3 Comprehensive Problem-to-Solution Mapping

| Persona | Pain point and operational breakdown | PractixOS core solution | Measurable business outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **New & returning patients** | Forced app downloads; uncertain wait times; opaque procedure fees; confusion over free follow-up rules | Zero-install web portal; automated WhatsApp booking; dynamic 7-day follow-up rule engine | Eliminates booking drop-off; removes billing arguments at reception |
| **Non-English speaking patients** | Cannot read English; intimidated by clinic communication; cannot follow complex post-operative care text | Localized Indic scripts (मराठी, తెలుగు, বাংলা, कोंकणी) with script-aware typography; automated 8-second regional WhatsApp voice notes | Zero communication drop-off; expands clinic reach into regional demographics |

### 2.4 Vertical Sequencing  *(new in v2.2)*

Versions 1.0 to 2.1 named **Dentistry and Mental Health Counselling** as co-equal Phase 1 verticals. Market sizing across the ten largest Indian metropolitan areas plus Goa does not support that pairing.

| Vertical | Practitioners in the target geography | Share | Status in v2.2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General practice & others | 247,435 | 57.7% | Deferred — see below |
| Dentistry | 80,168 | 18.7% | **Phase 1, sole launch vertical** |
| Obstetrics, gynaecology & IVF | 34,850 | 8.1% | Deferred — see below |
| Paediatrics | 27,880 | 6.5% | Phase 2, fourth |
| Ophthalmology | 17,425 | 4.1% | Phase 2, second |
| Dermatology | 13,940 | 3.3% | Phase 2, third |
| Mental health counselling | 6,970 | 1.6% | **Moved to Phase 2** |
| **Total** | **428,668** | | |

Mental health counselling is the smallest identified segment. In v2.1 it nonetheless consumed an entire milestone and the single hardest security subsystem in the product — the split-key encrypted vault. That is the correct investment eventually and the wrong investment first. The vault remains fully specified (FR-5.1 to FR-5.4, §4.4.4) and remains a genuine competitive moat; it ships in Phase 2.

**The sequence, and the reasoning behind it.** Ordering is by market size weighted against how much of the existing dental build each vertical reuses — because the cost of the *second* vertical determines whether there is ever a third.

1. **Dentistry (80,168).** The largest single specialty after undifferentiated general practice, with the deepest operational pain the product already addresses: physical chair constraints, external fabrication laboratories, visiting specialists and high-value multi-sitting treatment plans that are frequently abandoned. It is also the only vertical with a natural third-party distribution channel — see below.
2. **Ophthalmology (17,425).** The highest reuse of the dental build of any candidate. Its signature operational need, the multi-stage wait (*Waiting for Dilation* → *Ready for Doctor*), is satisfied entirely by the configurable queue stages of §4.6.1. More significantly, **optical dispensing maps almost one-to-one onto the dental laboratory work-order tracker**: an order carrying a prescription and a due date goes to an external lens laboratory, returns, and the patient is notified. The only genuinely new artefact is the refraction prescription format, which is a form rather than a subsystem.
3. **Dermatology (13,940).** The smallest of the three but the strongest unit economics in the market: cash-pay aesthetic work with no insurance friction, a short sales cycle, and practices that sit comfortably on the AI Enterprise tier rather than Solo Practice. It exercises package and series billing (§4.6.3) and batch-and-expiry inventory (§4.6.4), both of which dentistry also benefits from.
4. **Paediatrics (27,880).** The largest of the three and the most acute pain — 60 to 80 patients in a condensed evening session makes the Dynamic Token Radar a requirement rather than a differentiator — and immunisation recall is the strongest retention mechanism available anywhere in the product, because a missed immunisation is a clinical event rather than an inconvenience. It ranks fourth only because it is the one vertical requiring a genuine data-model change: **dependant and guardian accounts** (§4.6.5), since a parent books, consents and pays on behalf of a child.

**Deliberately deferred.**

* **Obstetrics, gynaecology and IVF (34,850)** is the largest deferred segment and the temptation is real. It is deferred because it needs a longitudinal nine-month care-cycle subsystem that has no analogue anywhere in the current product, it is imaging-heavy, and a substantial share of the segment practises inside hospitals rather than in the independent outpatient setting this product is built for.
* **General practice and others (247,435)** is 57.7% of the market and is deferred deliberately rather than dismissively. It is the least differentiated need, the lowest willingness to pay, and the segment every incumbent already contests. The correct approach is to arrive at it *last*, once queue stages, recalls, billing and inventory are all per-tenant configuration — at which point a general practice is a configuration file rather than a project. That same configurability is the precondition for any institutional or government deployment.

**Distribution note.** The dental laboratory is the product's most under-exploited commercial asset. The platform already creates direct value for laboratories (structured work orders carrying tooth number, shade and due date, reducing remakes), a single commercial laboratory serves between 100 and 300 dentists, and the relationship is already commercial. This is the cheapest distribution channel available at a ₹999–3,499 price point, where direct field sales cannot be afforded, and it should be tested before any sales hire.

### 3.2 Cost of Goods Sold and Margins  *(revised in v2.0)*

Operating margin is protected by two decisions: integrating directly with core platform APIs rather than paying reseller markups, and running an entirely free and open-source stack on infrastructure sized to actual load.

**Messaging — Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, India base tariffs**

* *Utility notifications* (confirmations, reschedules, vitals and receipt alerts): approximately ₹0.115 to ₹0.12 per message. A Clinic Pro practice processing 400 patient bookings a month incurs roughly 800 utility messages, or about **₹96 per month**.
* *24-hour customer service window:* free. When a patient initiates a query or taps an interactive button on a notification, all automated replies, triage questions and bot interactions for the next 24 hours cost ₹0. Conversational flows are deliberately designed to run inside this window.
* *Marketing broadcasts:* approximately ₹0.86 to ₹0.87 per message. Excluded from the base subscription and billed separately through pay-as-you-go credit packs.

**Hosting and database compute**

All data is stored in India to comply with DPDP Act sovereignty rules. Production runs on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure Always Free in `ap-mumbai-1` (Section 5).

| Stage | Infrastructure | Monthly infrastructure cost | Per clinic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot, 0–30 clinics | 1 × Ampere A1 (2 OCPU / 12 GB), Always Free | **₹0** | ₹0 |
| Growth, 30–150 clinics | Scale to A1 (4 OCPU / 24 GB) on pay-as-you-go | ~₹2,400 | ₹16–80 |
| Scale, 150–500 clinics | Separate database and application hosts plus load balancer | ~₹10,000 | ₹20–65 |
| 500+ clinics | Managed Postgres, read replica, multi-availability-domain | ~₹45,000 | ₹90 |

Every band clears the ₹250 per clinic target set in v1.0.

**Blended gross margin**

Total marginal delivery cost per active clinic, including messaging, payment gateway fees and support, falls between **₹100 and ₹250 per month**, against v1.0's estimate of ₹350 to ₹550. This lifts blended gross margin from 80–85% to **88% to 92%**.

### 3.3 The Leakage Recovery Model  *(new in v2.2 — the primary return-on-investment argument)*

Until v2.1 the commercial case rested on a government subsidy. Section 3.4 explains why it no longer can. The return-on-investment argument is now built on money the clinic is **already losing**, which has three advantages over a subsidy: it does not depend on policy, it is larger, and the platform can prove it after the fact rather than promise it in advance.

Section 2.1 establishes that urban outpatient no-show rates routinely exceed 20–30%. That is the single largest controllable loss in an independent practice, and it is joined by four smaller ones. PractixOS addresses all five, and — critically — **measures its own effect on each**, writing every recovery to an auditable ledger (FR-7.4).

| Leakage source | Mechanism that recovers it | Ledger event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patient forgets the appointment | Confirmed WhatsApp reminder in the patient's language, with a one-tap confirm | `no_show_prevented` |
| High-value slot booked casually, then abandoned | UPI reservation deposit at the time of booking (BR-03) | `deposit_converted` |
| Phased treatment plan silently abandoned after visit two | Recall engine re-contacts the patient at the clinically appropriate interval | `plan_resumed` |
| Lab remake caused by a missing shade or tooth number | Structured work order that cannot be submitted incomplete | `remake_avoided` |
| Consumable used chairside but never invoiced | Items logged in clinical notes are carried onto the invoice automatically | `consumable_billed` |

**Worked example — two-chair dental practice, 400 appointments per month, Clinic Pro at ₹1,999.** These are deliberately conservative per-clinic assumptions; the sales motion must quote these rather than the optimistic case.

| Event | Monthly volume | Unit value | Recovered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `no_show_prevented` | 5 | ₹800 | ₹4,000 |
| `deposit_converted` | 2 | ₹2,500 | ₹5,000 |
| `plan_resumed` | 1 | ₹6,000 | ₹6,000 |
| `remake_avoided` | 1 every 2 months | ₹1,400 | ₹700 |
| `consumable_billed` | 15 | ₹60 | ₹900 |
| | | **Total** | **₹16,600** |

The headline is not the total, which will vary widely by practice. The headline is the break-even: at an average realised appointment value of ₹800, **the subscription has paid for itself by the third recovered no-show of the month.** Everything after that is margin the clinic keeps.

**Anti-double-counting rule.** A single appointment may generate at most one attribution event. An appointment secured by a deposit is credited as `deposit_converted` and is explicitly *not* also credited as `no_show_prevented`, even where a reminder was delivered. Recovery totals that inflate under audit destroy the credibility of the entire number, and this figure exists precisely to survive an owner's scepticism.

**Why this is also the churn defence.** A subsidy is external; if the clinic cancels, the subsidy continues. Recovered revenue is not. The ledger is a running, evidenced statement of what stops happening the month the clinic leaves, and it is visible on the owner's dashboard every single month.

### 3.4 ABDM Compliance and the Digital Health Incentive Scheme  *(materially revised in v2.2)*

**The economics of this scheme changed in April 2026, and earlier versions of this document were wrong by roughly a factor of five.** Versions 1.0 to 2.1 stated that a clinic earns ₹20 per qualifying transaction and therefore around ₹9,000 a month, covering the subscription several times over. That is no longer true, and the claim must not appear in any sales material.

**Current rates — NHA Corrigendum 7 to the DHIS, effective April 2026** (`S-12021/070/2022-ABDM(Coord)`, published 09 April 2026):

| Recipient | Category 1 — Diagnostic Report, Discharge Summary | Category 2 — Prescription, OP Consultation, Immunization Record, Wellness Record, Invoice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health facility | ₹10 per eligible transaction | **₹5 per eligible transaction** |
| Digital Solution Company | ₹5 per eligible transaction | ₹2.50 per eligible transaction |

Four conditions materially reduce the realisable amount, and all four are new or tightened:

* **The first 100 transactions each month earn nothing.** The eligibility threshold is retained at 100 transactions, and those 100 are deducted before the incentive is computed.
* **Only KYC-verified ABHA-linked records qualify.** Scan & Share and Health Locker transactions no longer earn any incentive at all. In walk-in outpatient practice the KYC-verified fraction is well below 100%, and this is the largest single discount on the figure.
* **A 1-per-day and 5-per-month per-ABHA cap** applies to Category 2, limiting what a high-frequency returning patient can generate.
* **v3 API compliance is mandatory from July 2026.** A non-compliant facility or Digital Solution Company earns nothing, which makes the ABDM integration work a *gate* on any incentive rather than an enhancement to it.

*Corrected ROI impact.* An outpatient dental or counselling practice produces OP Consultations and Prescriptions. Those are **Category 2**. Taking the same 450 eligible bundles per month used in earlier versions, and assuming a favourable 90% KYC-verified rate:

> Clinic incentive = (450 × 0.90 − 100) × ₹5 = **₹1,525 per month**

against the ₹9,000 previously claimed. This does not cover the ₹1,999 Clinic Pro subscription. It is a useful contribution to it, and it should be sold as exactly that.

**Scheme durability.** DHIS has been amended seven times since its launch on 1 January 2023, and Corrigendum 7 is stated to be effective only to **September 2026**. The rate trend across those amendments is downward. No part of the commercial model may assume the scheme's continuation, its current rates, or its current eligibility rules. See risk R6, which this scheme has already triggered.

**What ABDM is genuinely for.** Interoperability and statutory compliance. Clinics will increasingly be required to produce ABDM-conformant records regardless of incentive, insurers and the National Health Claims Exchange consume the same FHIR artifacts, and a practice that cannot emit them will be progressively excluded from those flows. That is the durable argument, and it does not depend on a payment rate.

Accrual is recorded as a **ledger, not a counter**: every qualifying transaction is stored with its FHIR bundle reference, its Category 1 or 2 classification, and its KYC-verification status, so that a clinic's claim can be reconciled line by line against NHA records — and so that a future change to rates or categories is a repricing of existing rows rather than a rebuild.

### 4.3 Application & Client Tier Specifications

* **Frontend runtime.** Vite with React 18 and Tailwind CSS, built as a statically served, certified Progressive Web App. Server-side rendering is deliberately not used: every authenticated screen must render from IndexedDB when the network is down, so the client already owns rendering, and an SSR pass would produce a first paint the client must immediately reconcile. Public clinic portal pages (FR-1.1) are **pre-rendered to static HTML on profile change** so that they remain indexable.
* **Offline-first storage engine.** Local operational data — arrived patients, vitals entries, draft clinical notes, chart changes — is mirrored to IndexedDB using Dexie.js. When connectivity drops, staff continue working; changes sync through a background service worker on reconnect, and the pending queue is visible in the interface rather than hidden.
* **Conflict resolution.** Operational data resolves per field using last-write-wins with vector clocks. **Clinical notes never auto-merge**; conflicts are surfaced for explicit clinician resolution. Silent merging of clinical text is a patient-safety hazard.
* **Modular monolith.** One deployable with enforced module boundaries (`scheduling`, `clinical`, `vault`, `billing`, `messaging`, `abdm`, `sync`). Cross-module access goes through each module's exported service interface, never directly into another module's tables; a lint rule enforces this, preserving the option to extract a service later when load justifies it.
* **Responsive layout.** Minimum 48 × 48 CSS pixel touch targets throughout, rising to 64 × 64 in the nurse triage mode.

### 4.4 Data Architecture & Cryptographic Isolation

#### 4.4.1 Tenant isolation through Row-Level Security

Every tenant-scoped table carries `clinic_id`, and isolation is enforced by the database rather than by application code. Application-enforced tenancy fails the first time a query omits a `WHERE` clause; database-enforced tenancy cannot be forgotten.

```sql
ALTER TABLE patients ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY;

CREATE POLICY clinic_isolation ON patients
  USING (clinic_id = current_setting('app.clinic_id')::uuid);

-- BR-05 / FR-2.3: reception may read patients but never the real phone number.
REVOKE SELECT (phone_e164)   ON patients FROM app_reception;
GRANT  SELECT (phone_masked) ON patients TO   app_reception;
```

`phone_masked` is a generated column. Because column-level access to the real number is revoked from the reception database role, FR-2.3 holds even against a SQL-injection vector: the role genuinely cannot read the column.

Policies are created with `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` so that they apply to the table owner as well, and the application connects as a role that is not the owner. Without `FORCE`, the owning role bypasses every policy above and the entire isolation guarantee is decorative.

##### How `app.clinic_id` is set — the defect this prevents  *(new in v2.2)*

The policies above are only as strong as the mechanism that sets `app.clinic_id`, and the obvious implementation of that mechanism is wrong in a way that produces a silent, total cross-tenant breach.

The application uses a connection pool. `SET app.clinic_id = ...` is **session-scoped**: it persists on the physical connection after the request that issued it returns the connection to the pool. The next request to borrow that connection inherits the previous tenant's identifier, and if that request fails to set its own — an error path, an early return, a code path someone added later — it reads another clinic's data under a policy that believes it is doing the right thing.

```
  Request (tenant A)          Pooled connection #7          Request (tenant B)
        |                             |                             |
        |--- SET app.clinic_id = A -->|                             |
        |--- SELECT ... patients ---->|                             |
        |<-- A's rows. Correct. ------|                             |
        |--- release to pool -------->|                             |
        |                       [ setting survives ]                |
        |                             |<---- acquire connection ----|
        |                             |<---- SELECT ... patients ---|
        |                             |----- A's rows ------------->|
        |                             |                             |
        |                   BREACH. No error raised. ---------------+
```

This defect has three properties that make it unusually dangerous, and they are the reason it is specified here rather than left to implementation. It **passes every single-request test**, because with one request in flight the setting is always correct. It **produces no error**, only wrong rows. And it **grows more likely under load**, meaning it will first manifest in production during a busy clinic hour rather than in development.

Three controls are mandatory, and M0 does not exit without them:

1. **One accessor, using `SET LOCAL` inside an explicit transaction.** `SET LOCAL` is transaction-scoped and is discarded at commit or rollback, so releasing the connection cannot leak it.

   ```ts
   // The only sanctioned path from application code to tenant-scoped data.
   export async function withTenant<T>(
     clinicId: string,
     fn: (tx: PoolClient) => Promise<T>,
   ): Promise<T> {
     const tx = await pool.connect();
     try {
       await tx.query("BEGIN");
       // set_config's third argument = true means SET LOCAL. Parameterised,
       // because string-interpolating a tenant id into this statement would
       // hand an attacker the isolation boundary itself.
       await tx.query("SELECT set_config('app.clinic_id', $1, true)", [clinicId]);
       const result = await fn(tx);
       await tx.query("COMMIT");
       return result;
     } catch (err) {
       await tx.query("ROLLBACK");
       throw err;
     } finally {
       tx.release();
     }
   }
   ```

2. **A lint rule that makes the unsafe path unavailable.** `pool.query` and `pool.connect` are forbidden outside this module by an ESLint `no-restricted-properties` rule, with a single audited exemption for migrations and the pg-boss scheduler, which run outside any tenant context. A convention that relies on developers remembering is not a control; the point is that the wrong thing must fail the build.

3. **A CI gate that reproduces the breach conditions.** With `max: 1` on the pool, forcing every request onto the same physical connection, the test interleaves requests for two tenants and asserts that neither sees the other's rows. It additionally asserts that a request which *forgets* to set the context reads **zero rows** rather than the previous tenant's — which is the behaviour `SET LOCAL` gives for free, since an unset `app.clinic_id` matches nothing.

The background worker deserves explicit mention because it is the most likely place for this to be reintroduced: pg-boss jobs do not arrive with a request context, so every tenant-scoped job must carry its `clinic_id` in the payload and execute inside `withTenant`. A job that derives the tenant from anything other than its own payload is a defect.

See risk R12.

#### 4.4.2 BR-02 enforced by the database

Chair double-booking must be impossible, including when two receptionists book simultaneously. Between the `SELECT` that finds a chair free and the `INSERT` that books it, another transaction can do the same — so application-level checking cannot guarantee the rule. An exclusion constraint can:

```sql
CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS btree_gist;

ALTER TABLE appointments
  ADD CONSTRAINT chair_not_double_booked
  EXCLUDE USING gist (chair_id WITH =, during WITH &&)
  WHERE (chair_id IS NOT NULL AND state NOT IN ('cancelled','no_show'));

ALTER TABLE appointments
  ADD CONSTRAINT practitioner_not_double_booked
  EXCLUDE USING gist (practitioner_id WITH =, during WITH &&)
  WHERE (state NOT IN ('cancelled','no_show'));
```

Externally synced personal calendar blocks (FR-6.3) are inserted as appointments in a reserved state, so they participate in the same constraint automatically.

#### 4.4.3 BR-05 append-only audit trail

```sql
REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON audit_log FROM app_rw;
GRANT  INSERT, SELECT  ON audit_log TO   app_rw;
```

Even a fully compromised application cannot rewrite history. Retention and archival run under a separate role available only to a scheduled job. Every phone unmask, every vault open and every record export writes an entry.

#### 4.4.4 The clinical vault — corrected design

**The v1.0 design was not secure and is superseded.** FR-5.1 protects psychotherapy notes behind a 4-digit PIN with keys derived client-side via PBKDF2. The intent is right; the arithmetic is not. A 4-digit PIN has **10,000 possible values**. Against an attacker holding the database, no key derivation function saves you — a deliberately expensive KDF turns an instant break into a few hours of GPU time. The zero-knowledge property does not exist under that design.

Two changes make the promise real.

**Split the key.** Derive the encryption key from the PIN *and* a server-held secret released only to an authenticated, rate-limited session:

```
wrapping_key = Argon2id(
    password = user_pin || server_secret,
    salt     = per_note_salt,
    m = 64 MiB, t = 3, p = 1
)
```

`server_secret` lives in a separate keystore, never in the application database, and is released only after the therapist authenticates, subject to a hard rate limit and lockout. A stolen database dump then contains no brute-forceable material, because the attacker holds only half the key input. The therapist's experience is unchanged: they still type four digits.

**Use Argon2id, not PBKDF2.** OWASP has recommended Argon2id since 2021; PBKDF2 remains preferred only where FIPS certification is mandated, which does not apply here.

Additional controls: automatic re-lock after 10 minutes idle; keys held only in JavaScript closure memory and never in `localStorage`; every unlock and every failed attempt written to the audit log.

> **Note on the prototype.** `prototype/PractixOS.html` performs genuine encryption using the Web Crypto API, which exposes PBKDF2 but not Argon2id. It therefore uses PBKDF2 at 210,000 iterations and states the limitation on screen. Production uses Argon2id with the split key described above.

### 4.5 External Integration Adapters

* **Meta WhatsApp Cloud API.** Direct integration with Meta's Graph API for template registration, interactive webhook management and two-way conversational routing, bypassing reseller surcharges of 30 to 100 percent.
  *Critical path:* every utility template must be approved by Meta **per language** before it can be sent. Five languages across roughly a dozen template types is around sixty approvals, each taking days, with opaque rejections. Template submission is a week-one task, not a launch task.
* **NPCI / UPI payment adapter.** Instant UPI intent links for mobile web checkout, and dynamic time-limited UPI QR codes. **Every payment is reconciled against a server-side settlement webhook, never a client-side success callback**, since the client can disconnect or lie. *v2.2: webhook handling must be idempotent.* Gateways retry on any non-2xx response and on timeout, so the same settlement will arrive more than once; without protection this credits a deposit twice and corrupts both the day's reconciliation (FR-7.1) and the Recovered Revenue ledger (FR-7.4). Idempotency is enforced by a unique constraint on the provider's event identifier — inserting the event and applying its effect in one transaction, and treating a unique violation as a successful no-op — rather than by an application-level "have I seen this?" check, which races against itself under concurrent retries.
* **ABDM gateway connector.** M1 creates and verifies ABHA IDs via Aadhaar or mobile OTP. M2 serializes clinical visit summaries into HL7 FHIR profiles and pushes them to the national gateway. M3 manages consent requests for querying historical records. Bundles are constructed and validated with HAPI FHIR rather than assembled by hand, because profile conformance is where these integrations fail. Sandbox certification is a week-one task; lead time is measured in weeks and is outside our control.
* **Bi-directional calendar synchronization.** Google Calendar API v3 and Microsoft Graph, with PractixOS as master scheduler. Personal events block patient booking slots, and their titles are **masked at write time** to `"Doctor Unavailable (External Sync)"` — so the private title never enters clinic storage at all. This is a stronger guarantee than masking at read time: a database compromise cannot reveal a doctor's personal calendar.

### 4.6 The Configurable Vertical Engine  *(new in v2.2)*

§2.4 commits to four further verticals after dentistry. The economics of that commitment depend entirely on whether the second vertical costs a milestone or a configuration file. Examining what Ophthalmology, Dermatology, Paediatrics and Mental Health each actually need, the answer is that they need **variations of the same five things**. Built once as configuration they cost one milestone in total (M4); built per vertical they cost one milestone each.

| Subsystem | Dentistry needs | Ophthalmology | Dermatology | Paediatrics | Mental health |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Queue stages | 4 lanes | *Waiting for Dilation* | Patch-test wait | High-velocity list | Quiet single-room |
| Recalls | 6-month check-up | Annual refraction | Session intervals | Immunisation schedule | Weekly series |
| Packages | Multi-sitting plans | — | 6-session laser | — | Therapy blocks |
| Batch inventory | Consumables | Lens stock | Injectables, expiry-critical | Vaccine cold chain | — |
| Dependants | Family bookings | Elderly by proxy | — | **Mandatory** | Minors by guardian |

The governing rule: **no subsystem below may branch on `clinics.vertical`.** The vertical column selects a seed configuration at onboarding and is otherwise inert. Any `if vertical = 'x'` in application code is a defect, and the M4 exit criterion is precisely that a second vertical stands up with no code change at all.

#### 4.6.1 Per-tenant queue stages

FR-2.4's four lanes become rows. Ophthalmology's dilation wait, the single most-cited operational need in that vertical, is then a seed row rather than a feature.

```sql
CREATE TABLE queue_stages (
  id          uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  clinic_id   uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES clinics(id),
  position    int  NOT NULL,
  label_key   text NOT NULL,                        -- i18n key, not display text
  role        text NOT NULL CHECK (role IN          -- semantics the engine relies on
                 ('pre_arrival','waiting','in_service','post_service','settlement')),
  dwell_alert_mins int,                             -- amber past this, e.g. dilation 20 min
  UNIQUE (clinic_id, position)
);
```

`role` exists because some behaviour is genuinely semantic rather than cosmetic: arrival timestamps attach on the first transition into a `waiting` role, chair occupancy is computed from `in_service`, and the Recovered Revenue ledger treats reaching `post_service` as attendance. Labels and ordering are free; roles are a closed set. `dwell_alert_mins` is what makes a dilation wait operationally useful — the board flags a patient who has been dilating for 25 minutes.

#### 4.6.2 The recall engine

Named by the market analysis for at least four verticals, and currently reduced to a single WhatsApp marketing template. It is also the mechanism behind the `plan_resumed` event in FR-7.4, and therefore load-bearing for the commercial case.

```sql
CREATE TABLE recall_rules (
  id             uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  clinic_id      uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES clinics(id),
  trigger        text NOT NULL CHECK (trigger IN
                   ('procedure_completed','plan_dormant','age_months','last_visit')),
  trigger_ref    text,                              -- procedure code, or null
  offset_days    int  NOT NULL,
  template_key   text NOT NULL,
  max_attempts   int  NOT NULL DEFAULT 2,
  active         boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true
);

CREATE TABLE recalls (
  id          uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  clinic_id   uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES clinics(id),
  patient_id  uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES patients(id),
  rule_id     uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES recall_rules(id),
  due_on      date NOT NULL,
  state       text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'pending'
                CHECK (state IN ('pending','sent','booked','declined','expired')),
  attempts    int  NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  UNIQUE (clinic_id, patient_id, rule_id, due_on)    -- idempotent generation
);
CREATE INDEX ON recalls (clinic_id, due_on) WHERE state = 'pending';
```

A pg-boss scheduled job materialises due recalls and dispatches them. The `age_months` trigger is what makes a paediatric immunisation schedule expressible — a rule per vaccine, offset from date of birth — without a line of paediatrics-specific code. The unique constraint matters more than it looks: a scheduled job that runs twice must not message a patient twice.

#### 4.6.3 Package and series billing

A prepaid block of sessions that draws down per visit. Dermatology's six-session laser course, mental health's therapy block and dentistry's multi-sitting plan are the same object.

```sql
CREATE TABLE package_definitions (
  id            uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  clinic_id     uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES clinics(id),
  name          text NOT NULL,
  sessions      int  NOT NULL CHECK (sessions > 0),
  price_paise   bigint NOT NULL,
  valid_days    int,                                -- null = no expiry
  min_gap_days  int                                 -- clinical interval between sessions
);

CREATE TABLE patient_packages (
  id             uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  clinic_id      uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES clinics(id),
  patient_id     uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES patients(id),
  definition_id  uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES package_definitions(id),
  purchased_at   timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  expires_on     date,
  sessions_used  int NOT NULL DEFAULT 0
);

ALTER TABLE patient_packages
  ADD CONSTRAINT no_overdraw CHECK (sessions_used >= 0);
```

Redemption is a row in a `package_redemptions` table referencing the appointment, with `sessions_used` maintained by trigger rather than by the application, so that two concurrent front-desk redemptions cannot both succeed against a single remaining session. The remaining balance is visible on the patient portal and at the front desk, because a patient who cannot see what they have left will dispute it.

#### 4.6.4 Batch and expiry inventory

Roadmap feature 6 specifies auto-depletion against a count and a buffer. That is sufficient for a rubber dam sheet and insufficient for a botulinum toxin vial, a vaccine or a contact lens solution, where the batch number is a regulatory traceability requirement and the expiry date is a patient-safety one.

```sql
CREATE TABLE inventory_batches (
  id            uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  clinic_id     uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES clinics(id),
  item_id       uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES inventory_items(id),
  batch_no      text NOT NULL,
  expires_on    date NOT NULL,
  qty_received  int  NOT NULL CHECK (qty_received > 0),
  qty_remaining int  NOT NULL CHECK (qty_remaining >= 0),
  UNIQUE (clinic_id, item_id, batch_no)
);
CREATE INDEX ON inventory_batches (clinic_id, expires_on) WHERE qty_remaining > 0;
```

Depletion consumes the earliest-expiring batch with stock remaining, and every consumption records the batch against the clinical encounter — which is what makes a product recall answerable. Expiry alerting reuses the recall engine's scheduled job rather than adding a second scheduler.

#### 4.6.5 Dependants, guardians and consent by proxy

Specified now, ahead of the paediatric vertical, because retrofitting proxy consent into a live DPDP-regulated schema is disproportionately expensive and because dental families book together from day one.

```sql
CREATE TABLE patient_relationships (
  id            uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  clinic_id     uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES clinics(id),
  guardian_id   uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES patients(id),
  dependant_id  uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES patients(id),
  relationship  text NOT NULL CHECK (relationship IN
                  ('parent','legal_guardian','spouse','carer')),
  may_book      boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true,
  may_consent   boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,     -- deliberately off by default
  may_view_clinical boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,
  valid_until   date,                               -- e.g. the dependant's 18th birthday
  CHECK (guardian_id <> dependant_id),
  UNIQUE (clinic_id, guardian_id, dependant_id)
);
```

Four obligations attach to this table, and none of them are optional:

* **A dependant is a full patient record**, never a field on the guardian. A child has their own clinical history, their own ABHA identifier and their own immunisation schedule, and will one day have their own account.
* **The three capabilities are independent.** Booking on someone's behalf is routine; consenting to a procedure on their behalf is a legal act; reading their clinical record is a third thing again. `may_consent` and `may_view_clinical` default to false and must be granted explicitly.
* **Proxy consent is recorded as proxy consent.** Every consent captured under this relationship stores the guardian's identity, the relationship type and the relationship row as it stood at that moment. A consent record that merely says "the patient agreed" when a parent agreed is not a defensible record under the DPDP Act.
* **Expiry is enforced, not advisory.** `valid_until` passing revokes the capabilities. A guardian relationship over a minor must not silently persist past majority; the platform surfaces the transition and requires the now-adult patient to re-establish any continuing access.

Guardian phone numbers are shared across dependants by design, which means the `UNIQUE (clinic_id, phone_e164)` constraint on `patients` must be relaxed to accommodate a family on one number. It is replaced by a partial unique index on self-managing patients only, with the guardian's number carried on the relationship rather than implied by equality of phone numbers.

## 5. Hosting, Infrastructure & Cost Control  *(new in v2.0)*

**Decision: production runs on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure Always Free in `ap-mumbai-1`. Microsoft for Startups Azure credits fund workloads that are expensive, time-boxed and disposable, and never the production path.**

### 5.1 Why production does not run on the Azure credits

The Microsoft for Startups self-serve offer is $1,000 valid for 90 days, followed by a further $4,000 for 180 days after business verification — roughly ₹4.4 lakh across about nine months, after which the subscription converts automatically to pay-as-you-go. Investor Network-backed startups begin at $100,000 with up to $150,000 available over two years.

Either way, credits are a subsidy with an expiry date. Putting the production database of a clinical system on an expiring subsidy guarantees a migration under deadline pressure at exactly the moment the business is trying to grow. And the commercial pitch in Section 3.3 requires a defensible cost-per-clinic figure; "currently free because Microsoft is paying" is not that figure.

If the Investor Network offer is available, take it, and spend it as described in 5.5.

### 5.2 What OCI Always Free provides

Verified against Oracle's current documentation, September 2026:

| Resource | Always Free allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Ampere A1 compute (Arm) | 1,500 OCPU-hours + 9,000 GB-hours per month, i.e. **2 OCPU / 12 GB** sustained |
| AMD micro instances | 2 × VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro (1/8 OCPU, 1 GB) |
| Block storage | 200 GB total |
| Object storage | 10 GB |
| Outbound data transfer | **10 TB per month** |
| Load balancer | 1 flexible, 10 Mbps |

Oracle **halved** the Ampere allowance in 2026, from 4 OCPU / 24 GB to 2 / 12; plan against the current figure, since some Oracle pages still display the old numbers. The 10 TB egress allowance is unusually generous and removes the cost line that most often surprises teams on AWS.

### 5.3 Three setup facts that will otherwise cost days

1. **Upgrade to pay-as-you-go before provisioning anything.** Ampere A1 capacity is effectively unobtainable on a Free Tier account; "Out of host capacity" is the normal experience, and operators report retry scripts failing for months. On a pay-as-you-go account the Always Free allowance remains free, but you gain capacity priority and exemption from idle-resource reclamation. The card on file is charged only for usage beyond the free allowance.
2. **The home region cannot be changed after signup on a free account.** Select `ap-mumbai-1`, which is confirmed to offer `VM.Standard.A1.Flex`. This thirty-second choice determines whether the product is DPDP-compliant, and it is irreversible.
3. **Oracle does terminate free accounts.** A pay-as-you-go account with a valid card sharply reduces the risk; a tested restore into a second provider is the actual insurance.

### 5.4 Production topology

```
ap-mumbai-1
├── VM.Standard.A1.Flex  (2 OCPU / 12 GB / 150 GB)   [Always Free]
│   ├── caddy       TLS termination, Coraza WAF, static PWA, reverse proxy
│   ├── api         Fastify, Node 22 (arm64)
│   ├── postgres    PostgreSQL 16 + pg-boss queues
│   └── netdata     lightweight metrics
│
├── VM.Standard.E2.1.Micro (1 GB)                    [Always Free]
│   └── off-host uptime probe + weekly restore-drill runner
│
├── Object Storage (10 GB)                           [Always Free]
│   └── pgBackRest WAL archive + base backups
│
└── Azure Blob, Central India                        [credits, then ~₹200/mo]
    └── geo-redundant encrypted nightly dumps
```

No Kubernetes. Kubernetes on a single host is pure overhead, and OKE's free control plane does not change that because the worker nodes are the cost and the complexity.

The micro instance earns its keep by living **outside** the main host: an uptime probe running on the machine it monitors is not a probe.

### 5.5 Where the Azure credits go

The governing rule: **anything running on credits must be movable in an afternoon.** Docker Compose everywhere, no proprietary Azure PaaS in the critical path.

| Workload | Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Geo-redundant encrypted backups (Blob, Central India) | High value, low cost, worth paying for after credits lapse |
| Staging and demo environments (Azure VM running the production Docker Compose file) | Genuinely disposable. *Corrected in v2.2: originally specified as Container Apps. A staging environment on a different runtime to production cannot catch what staging exists to catch — Compose networking, volume behaviour, resource limits and container startup ordering are precisely where single-host deployments break. Staging runs the same `compose.yml` as production, on a smaller VM.* |
| Load and soak testing before pilot go-live | Burst compute is exactly what credits are for |
| **GPU time to fine-tune IndicWhisper for Speech-to-Rx** | The highest-value use available: compute we would never otherwise buy, producing model weights — a durable asset that outlives the credits |
| Azure OpenAI / Foundry for AI Enterprise tier research | Time-boxed R&D |
| ~~Production database~~ | Never |
| ~~Production application~~ | Never |
| ~~Production on any non-Indian edge, including Vercel~~ | Never. *Added in v2.2.* `prototype/PractixOS.html` is deployed to Vercel for pilot feedback, which is appropriate: it carries fictional data and no backend. That deployment must not become the production path. Vercel terminates TLS outside India, which would contradict the data-residency argument in §8.2 that the entire architecture — Caddy in Mumbai, OCI `ap-mumbai-1`, DNS as the only third party in the request path — exists to support |

### 5.6 Availability, backup and recovery

The availability requirement is restated in Section 8.2 as **99.9% clinic-perceived availability**, with an infrastructure recovery time objective of 20 minutes and a recovery point objective of 5 minutes. Three things make that claim honest, and none of them is optional:

1. **Infrastructure as code.** Terraform plus cloud-init for the entire host, so a rebuild is a twenty-minute automated process rather than a day of remembering.
2. **Continuous archiving.** pgBackRest with WAL archiving to OCI Object Storage, giving a five-minute recovery point.
3. **A weekly automated restore drill** that provisions a throwaway container, restores the most recent backup, verifies checksums and row counts, reports to the team channel, and destroys itself. On single-host infrastructure, an unverified backup is not a backup.

## 6. Experience Architecture & The "Clinical Ink" Design System  *(new in v2.0)*

Version 1.0 specified what the software must do but not what it is like to use. In a market where the competing products are functionally similar and the buyers are time-poor, that is the gap where the product is won or lost.

### 6.1 Design thesis

The visual identity derives from what an Indian clinic actually is: **warm paper, teal ink, marigold for urgency, and statuses as stamped marks.** Not sky-blue-on-cool-grey, which is what every incumbent looks like, and which reads to an Indian clinician as foreign software.

### 6.2 Six role modes, one spine

The same system presents itself in the register each role needs. Role-adaptive modes are the central interaction concept and the hardest part of the design to copy, because each mode encodes an observation about the working conditions of the person using it.

| Mode | Role | Design response | Because |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Board** | Reception | Air-traffic-control density; drag targets; wait time expressed as colour temperature | The receptionist manages a room, not a record; she needs to perceive state peripherally |
| **The Slate** | Triage nurse | 64px targets, numeric keypads, one question at a time | Gloves and hurry; the OS keyboard costs two seconds and an accuracy penalty |
| **Chairside Console** | Dentist | Dark theme, luminous chart | The monitor sits on a swing arm under operatory lighting, in peripheral vision. No competitor ships this |
| **Quiet Room** | Therapist | Warm paper, serif type, 66-character measure, no badges or counters | A counselling record is written slowly and read carefully; urgency cues are actively harmful |
| **Route Card** | Visiting consultant | Phone-first vertical timeline, thumb-zone action bar | Used standing up between clinics: where next, what am I owed |
| **The Doorway** | Patient | Oversized type, one decision per screen, language chosen first, voice always within reach | Used once, under stress, possibly by someone who does not read comfortably |

### 6.3 Typography

* **Two registers.** A dense staff face optimised for information density, and a generous patient face at a larger scale with wider leading and a constrained measure. Same family, deliberately different rhythm.
* **Script-aware Indic typography.** Devanagari, Telugu and Bengali each receive their own font stack and their own line-height. A global `line-height: 1.5` clips Telugu and Bengali conjuncts — a real defect in the v1.0 reference prototype, and the kind that only surfaces after shipping to a Bengali-speaking clinic.
* **Tabular numerals** everywhere numbers are compared in a column: fees, vitals, tokens, ledger lines.
* Fonts must be self-hosted (Noto Sans Devanagari / Telugu / Bengali, SIL Open Font License) rather than loaded from a third-party CDN, both for offline capability and for data residency. *Restated as a build requirement in v2.2, because the prototype does not yet satisfy it.* Relying on a system font stack is not self-hosting. The devices this product explicitly targets — low-cost Android handsets and ageing Windows reception machines — frequently ship without Telugu or Bengali installed, and the failure mode is that the flagship localisation renders as rows of empty boxes on precisely the hardware it was built for. Concretely:
  * Ship **subsetted `woff2`** files per script, built from the Google Fonts sources at package time and checked into the deployment artifact. Full Noto Devanagari is several hundred kilobytes; a subset covering the glyphs the product actually emits is a fraction of that, which matters on a 4G connection.
  * **Load per language, never all at once.** A clinic operating in English and Marathi must not download Telugu and Bengali.
  * Declare `unicode-range` on each `@font-face` so the browser fetches a script only when it encounters it, and `font-display: swap` so a slow font never blanks the queue board.
  * The same files serve the print stylesheet of FR-4.6.

### 6.4 Interaction rules

* **Undo, not confirm.** Financial and destructive actions apply immediately with an eight-second undo. Confirmation dialogs are fatal to a 15-second workflow, and high-turnover staff learn to dismiss them reflexively — which makes them slower *and* less safe.
* **Never more than three levels.** Role, screen, modal. Nested menus are named in Section 2.2 as a specific failure of the incumbents.
* **Keypads, never keyboards** for vitals, fees and tooth numbers.
* **Colour is never the only signal.** Every state carries shape, pattern and text. Roughly 8% of Indian men have a colour vision deficiency, and FR-4.2 as written in v1.0 encoded diagnosis in colour alone.
* **Optimistic writes with a visible queue.** Actions land instantly; the sync ledger shows what is pending. Offline becomes legible rather than frightening.
* **Motion is functional only.** 150–200 ms, and `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured.

### 6.5 On defensibility

The stylesheet is copyable in an afternoon, and claiming otherwise would be dishonest. What resists copying is the reasoning — why the dentist gets a dark theme and the therapist gets warm paper, why undo beats confirm, why the wait-time gradient exists. A copy made without the reasoning reads as a copy.

The durable moats are elsewhere and should be invested in accordingly: **ABDM certification**, the **approved Meta template library** across five languages, the **dental laboratory network**, and the **chair-constraint scheduling logic**.

### 6.6 The breakpoint ladder *(new in v2.1)*

Three layout modes, not a spectrum. Each one is a deliberate density decision rather than an arbitrary width.

| Mode | Applies | Navigation | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Compact** | below 600 px | Bottom tab bar, five slots, the fifth being an overflow sheet | One queue lane at a time behind a segmented control; the odontogram becomes a quadrant selector; tables render as card lists; modals become bottom sheets; the owner's week becomes a vertical list |
| **Tablet** | 600–1023 px | Icon rail, no labels | Two queue lanes; the full dental arch returns; real tables return; centred dialogs replace bottom sheets |
| **Full** | 1024 px and above | Labelled rail plus header controls | Four queue lanes; the visiting consultant's route card gains a phone bezel, which is honest signalling that the screen was designed for a pocket |

Two implementation notes, both of which follow from the first.

**Layout is driven by an attribute, not by media queries.** A `data-lay` attribute on the root element carries the mode, and every responsive rule keys off it. The mode is normally computed from the viewport, but because the mechanism is an attribute rather than a media query, pinning it costs one assignment and duplicates no CSS. This is what makes the per-device Compact / Tablet / Full override affordable, and it is also what lets the layout checker force each mode deterministically instead of inferring it from a window size that headless Chrome refuses to honour below roughly 500 px.

**A component that diverges still shares its vocabulary.** The reception board on a phone and on a tablet are different layouts, but the card inside them is byte-identical: same name, same token, same rubber-stamp alert, same actions in the same order. A receptionist trained on the tablet is not re-learning the product on her phone; she is seeing less of it at once.

## 7. Business Requirements Document (BRD)

### 7.1 Project Scope & Target Markets

* **Phase 1 launch verticals.** Independent dental practices (solo BDS and multi-chair clinics) and private mental health practitioners (psychologists and counsellors).
* **Geographic focus.** Urban and semi-urban medical clusters in India: Bengaluru, Pune, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Kolkata and Tier-2 medical hubs.
* **Phase 2 expansion.** Multi-specialty outpatient polyclinics, paediatric chambers and general physician practices.

### 7.2 Stakeholder Ecosystem & Business Objectives

* **Clinic owners.** Protect operatory margins, reduce front-desk cash leakage, minimise no-shows, and eliminate per-seat software fees.
* **Associate dentists and visiting MDS consultants.** Clear automated fee-split ledgers and synchronized external schedules to avoid double-booking.
* **Reception and nursing staff.** Move away from paper registers and speed up check-in without learning complex software.
* **Patients.** Zero-friction booking, native-language communication, transparent fees, automated free follow-ups.
* **Commercial dental laboratories.** Clear digital work orders that reduce remake errors and streamline billing.

### 7.3 Business Rules Engine

**BR-01 — Dynamic follow-up rule policy**
* The system checks the patient's visit history for the selected doctor and specialty.
* If a primary consultation was completed within the last *N* calendar days (default 7, configurable between 3 and 15), the consultation fee calculates as **₹0, complimentary follow-up**.
* On day *N*+1 the fee resets to the standard consultation rate, eliminating front-desk billing disputes.
* *v2.0 addition:* the reason for the computed fee is stored on the appointment (`fee_reason`), so a dispute can be settled by reading the record rather than by argument.

**BR-02 — Multi-chair resource dependency (dentistry)**
* Booking an in-clinic dental appointment requires satisfying a three-way dependency: clinician availability AND operatory chair availability AND specialized hardware availability.
* Overlapping bookings for the same operatory chair are strictly prevented, even when multiple doctors are on site.
* *v2.0 addition:* enforced by a PostgreSQL exclusion constraint (Section 4.4.2), so the rule holds under concurrent booking rather than only under sequential booking.

**BR-03 — Visiting consultant fee-split settlement**
* Consultant earnings calculate automatically from gross collections minus agreed deductions:

  > Net payout = (gross procedure fee − dental lab cost − surgical consumables) × agreed doctor split (60%)

* The payout updates the visiting specialist's mobile ledger in real time on payment confirmation.

**BR-04 — Psychotherapy pre-payment and cancellation policy**
* In-person and virtual psychotherapy bookings require a 100% upfront UPI deposit.
* Cancellations within 24 hours of the scheduled start forfeit the deposit, compensating for reserved clinical time.
* Patients are automatically offered conversion of an in-person session to a video call rather than forfeiting.
* *v2.0 addition:* the policy is stated in the patient's own language **before** payment, not only in terms and conditions afterwards.

**BR-05 — DPDP Act 2023 statutory privacy compliance**
* Front-desk accounts display masked phone numbers, showing only the last two digits, preventing unapproved export of patient lists.
* Every record access, edit and export is logged in an append-only audit trail.
* Mental health session formulations remain inaccessible to administrative staff.
* *v2.0 addition:* masking is enforced by a column-level `REVOKE` on the reception database role, and the audit trail by revoking `UPDATE` and `DELETE` from the application role (Section 4.4). Both rules become structural rather than conventional.

## 8. Product Requirements Document (PRD)

### 8.1 Functional Requirements

#### Module 1: Patient Discovery & Booking Experience

* **FR-1.1** — **Tenant workspaces.** Generate a sovereign, white-labelled web URL for each clinic (e.g. `drsharma.practix.in`) requiring zero app downloads. *Expanded in v2.1:*
  * **FR-1.1.1 — Resolution.** The tenant is resolved from the request host before authentication and before any row is read. The resolved tenant is bound to the session; there is no cross-tenant view, no "all clinics" mode, and no super-user list, at any tier of the product.
  * **FR-1.1.2 — Isolation.** Every table carrying clinical or commercial data holds a `clinic_id` and is governed by a PostgreSQL row-level security policy with `FORCE` enabled (Section 4.4). A query for a record belonging to another tenant returns **zero rows, not an authorization error** — a denial confirms the record exists, which would itself let one clinic test whether a patient attends another.
  * **FR-1.1.3 — Configuration.** Display name, mark, accent colour, enabled languages, the free-follow-up window, the room and chair registry, and the enabled module set are per-tenant columns. Two tenants may therefore return different fee outcomes under BR-01 for the same patient on the same day, and both are correct.
  * **FR-1.1.4 — Module provisioning by vertical.** A dental workspace does not expose the Mental Health Confidentiality Vault; a counselling practice does not expose the odontogram or the nurse triage desk; a polyclinic exposes both. Modules absent from a tenant's vertical are not merely hidden in navigation — they are not provisioned.
  * **FR-1.1.5 — Membership.** A practitioner working across clinics, such as a visiting MDS, holds one membership per tenant and switches between them explicitly. There is no merged inbox and no combined calendar view; the fee-split ledger aggregates the practitioner's own earnings, never another clinic's patient data.
  * **FR-1.1.6 — Onboarding and exit.** A new tenant is provisioned by inserting one clinic row and its configuration; no schema change, no new database and no new deployment. Exit is symmetrical: a per-tenant logical export for portability, and a per-tenant erasure job satisfying DPDP deletion requests without touching neighbouring tenants.
  * **FR-1.1.7 — Fair use.** Rate limits are applied per tenant at the edge, and a statement timeout is applied to the application database role, so one clinic running a heavy report cannot degrade another.
* **FR-1.2** — The public portal supports on-the-fly language switching across English, Marathi, Telugu, Bengali and Konkani. *v2.0: this covers every patient-facing surface — confirmations, receipts, care notes and queue updates — not only the landing page.*
* **FR-1.3** — Natural-language matching for symptoms, clinical terms and doctor names across all supported Indic languages.
* **FR-1.4** — Patient authentication via WhatsApp OTP, eliminating username and password setup.
* **FR-1.5** — The booking flow presents real-time open slots, confirms consultation mode (in-clinic or video), and applies follow-up fee rules before checkout.
* **FR-1.6** — **Self-service onboarding.** *(New in v2.2.)* A clinic reaches its first live booking without human assistance. FR-1.1.6 already establishes that provisioning a tenant is one row insert; this requirement covers the path a clinic owner actually walks.
  * A guided wizard collects, in order: clinic identity and slug; the vertical, which seeds queue stages, recall rules and the module set per §4.6 rather than branching code; practitioners; chairs or rooms; working hours and break windows; a fee schedule; and the enabled language set.
  * **Every step is skippable and resumable.** An owner interrupted by a patient must be able to close the browser and return. Nothing collected in the wizard is unavailable later in settings.
  * The wizard terminates in a **live test booking against the clinic's own portal**, because the moment of conviction is seeing a real WhatsApp confirmation arrive, not reaching the end of a form.
  * The target is a working clinic in under fifteen minutes with no call, no email and no implementation consultant. At the scale §2.4 contemplates, hand-configuration is not merely expensive; it is the constraint on growth.
* **FR-1.7** — **Data import.** *(New in v2.2.)* Every clinic worth winning already keeps patients somewhere — a spreadsheet, Dentee, Practo or a paper register transcribed into Excel. A practice that cannot bring them runs two systems in parallel, and within a quarter runs only the old one.
  * CSV and Excel upload with **interactive column mapping**, including reasonable automatic guesses for common headings in English and transliterated forms.
  * A **dry run is mandatory before any write**: the importer reports rows that will be created, rows that will be merged against an existing patient, and rows that will be rejected with the reason, and the owner confirms before anything is committed.
  * Phone numbers are normalised to E.164 and duplicates are surfaced for a merge decision rather than silently unified, since two patients genuinely can share a household number (§4.6.5).
  * The import runs as one transaction per batch under the tenant context of FR-1.1.2, is fully reversible for 24 hours, and writes a summary to the audit trail.

#### Module 2: Front-Desk Reception & Queue Management

* **FR-2.1** — A single-screen, real-time queue board whose columns are the tenant's configured stages (§4.6.1), seeded for dentistry as *Expected Today*, *Waiting Room*, *In Consultation*, *Billing / Exit*. *v2.2: at high consultation velocity — a paediatric evening session running sixty to eighty patients — the board offers an equivalent compact list presentation with search, because a card grid stops being legible well before that volume.*
* **FR-2.2** — Walk-in patient creation requires only two inputs, mobile number and patient name, creating the record and a queue token in under 10 seconds. *v2.2: the token is issued **per practitioner**, not once per clinic per day. A patient in a three-doctor polyclinic cares about their position in Dr. Rao's queue; a clinic-wide token number tells them nothing and is actively misleading when one practitioner runs late and another does not.*
* **FR-2.3** — Front-desk views mask patient phone numbers by default (e.g. `+91 98220•••10`), with unmasking restricted to authorized administrative roles and written to the audit trail.
* **FR-2.4** — Moving a patient card between columns immediately updates the doctor's chamber screen and waiting-room displays. *v2.1: drag is implemented on Pointer Events, not HTML5 drag-and-drop, so it behaves identically under a mouse, a stylus and a thumb. Every card also carries an equivalent non-drag path — a "Move to…" sheet — because on a phone only one lane is on screen and there is nowhere to drag to.* *v2.2: the columns themselves are per-tenant configuration rather than four fixed lanes (§4.6.1). A stage may carry a dwell alert, so an ophthalmology board can flag a patient who has been dilating for longer than expected.*

#### Module 3: Nurse Triage & Clinical Vitals Desk

* **FR-3.1** — A high-contrast interface to log baseline vitals (blood pressure, pulse, blood sugar, weight) in under 10 seconds, using numeric keypads rather than the OS keyboard.
* **FR-3.2** — Medical alert tags (penicillin allergy, local anaesthetic reaction, hypertension risk, diabetes, bleeding risk, pregnancy) displaying high-visibility banners across all clinical screens.

#### Module 4: Dental Specialty Chamber Console

* **FR-4.1** — An interactive 32-tooth graphical odontogram using FDI two-digit numbering. *v2.1: the full upper and lower arch is presented at 600 px and above. Below that, a quadrant selector (UR / UL / LR / LL) presents eight teeth at a time, because sixteen teeth across a 375 px screen gives each target roughly 21 px against a 48 px accessibility floor. Notation, states and behaviour are identical in both presentations; only the number of teeth on screen changes.*
* **FR-4.2** — Tooth states: healthy, caries, RCT needed or done, crown or prosthetic, missing. *v2.0: each state is encoded by colour **and** fill pattern **and** text label; colour alone is never the signal.*
* **FR-4.3** — Selecting clinical conditions auto-populates a phased, multi-sitting treatment plan with procedural steps, estimates, completed treatments and remaining balance.
* **FR-4.4** — A digital dental lab work-order tracker recording assigned lab, tooth number, shade guide, restoration type and delivery date.
* **FR-4.5** — One-click dispatch of structured work orders to lab managers over WhatsApp. *v2.2: the order cannot be dispatched with tooth number, restoration type or shade missing. This is the control that makes the `remake_avoided` ledger event (FR-7.4.1) evidentiary rather than assumed, and it is also the whole of the value the platform offers the laboratory — see the distribution note in §2.4.*
* **FR-4.6** — **Print artifacts.** *(New in v2.2.)* Indian outpatient practice is substantially paper-terminated: the patient leaves with a prescription in hand and frequently requires a printed receipt for reimbursement. A product with no print path will be asked for one in the first week of the first pilot.
  * Prescription and payment receipt, each rendering correctly on **A5 and A4**, which are the two sizes clinic printers are actually loaded with.
  * Clinic letterhead from the tenant configuration of FR-1.1.3: display name, mark, registration number and address.
  * **Indic-script safe.** Print uses the same self-hosted subsetted fonts as the screen (§6.6), because a prescription that renders as boxes at the pharmacy counter is worse than no prescription.
  * Rendered from the same document the patient receives over WhatsApp, so the printed and digital artifacts cannot diverge.
  * Print is a browser print stylesheet, not a server-side PDF service. It costs nothing to run, works offline, and adds no dependency to the production path.

#### Module 5: Mental Health Confidentiality Vault

* **FR-5.1** — Psychotherapy notes locked behind an encrypted vault requiring a secondary 4-digit PIN. *v2.0: the key is derived with Argon2id from the PIN combined with a server-held secret released only to a rate-limited authenticated session (Section 4.4.4). The vault is locked by default and re-locks after 10 minutes idle.*
* **FR-5.2** — When locked, only demographic and payment metadata is visible; all clinical text is masked from front-desk staff. There is no administrator override, because there is no server-side key capable of decryption alone.
* **FR-5.3** — Structured SOAP progress-note templates, DSM-5 diagnostic coding, and GAD-7 / PHQ-9 psychometric tracking with trend display.
* **FR-5.4** — Automated weekly recurring reservations linked to upfront UPI pre-payment.

#### Module 6: Visiting MDS Specialist & Calendar Sync Hub

* **FR-6.1** — A unified mobile view of multi-clinic itineraries without multiple logins.
* **FR-6.2** — Bi-directional synchronization with Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook.
* **FR-6.3** — Personal calendar events automatically block clinical availability with titles masked from receptionists. *v2.0: masked at write time, so the private title never enters clinic storage.*
* **FR-6.4** — An automated fee-split ledger tracking gross billings, lab and consumable deductions, and net payouts.
* **FR-6.5** — **Two-sided settlement.** *(New in v2.2.)* The consultant can request settlement; the owner must be able to respond to it. A settlement moves through `requested` → `approved` → `paid`, or `requested` → `disputed`, with the owner's view itemising the same gross, deduction and net lines the consultant sees.
  * A dispute attaches to specific ledger lines rather than to the settlement as a whole, so the undisputed remainder can still be paid.
  * Every transition is written to the audit trail with the acting identity, because the purpose of this requirement is to replace an argument conducted over WhatsApp with a record neither party can revise afterwards.
  * Payment itself remains outside the platform; PractixOS records agreement, not the transfer.

#### Module 7: Clinic Administration, Recovered Revenue & ABDM Compliance

* **FR-7.1** — Reconcile daily collections, balancing physical cash handovers against digital UPI settlements.
* **FR-7.2** — Track generated ABHA records, M2 clinical pushes, and accrued DHIS incentive in real time, as a reconcilable ledger rather than a counter. *v2.2: each row additionally stores its Category 1 or Category 2 classification and its KYC-verification status, and the accrual formula applies the 100-transaction monthly exclusion, so that a rate or category change is a repricing of existing rows rather than a rebuild (§3.4).*
* **FR-7.3** — A chair-time profitability calculator analysing fixed clinic overhead against procedure duration to display net margin per chair-minute. *v2.0: computed against minutes actually utilised as well as minutes available, since the two produce materially different rates and utilisation is the lever the owner controls.*
* **FR-7.4** — **The Recovered Revenue ledger.** *(New in v2.2. This is the requirement that carries the product's commercial case; see §3.3.)* The platform records, as an append-only ledger, every instance in which it demonstrably recovered revenue the practice would otherwise have lost.

  **FR-7.4.1 — Event types.** Exactly five events may be written, each only when its evidentiary precondition is met:

  | Event | Written when | Credited value |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `no_show_prevented` | A reminder was *delivered* (provider receipt, not merely queued), the patient confirmed, and the appointment was subsequently attended | Realised fee for the appointment |
  | `deposit_converted` | A UPI reservation deposit was collected at booking (BR-03) and the appointment was attended | Realised fee for the appointment |
  | `plan_resumed` | A recall was delivered against a treatment plan dormant beyond its clinical interval, and a booking followed within the attribution window | Value of the plan phase subsequently delivered |
  | `remake_avoided` | A lab work order was submitted carrying complete tooth number, restoration type and shade, and was accepted without a remake request | The practice's configured remake cost |
  | `consumable_billed` | An item logged against the clinical encounter was carried onto the invoice by the platform rather than entered manually | Line value of the item |

  **FR-7.4.2 — Evidence.** Every row stores the identifier of the artefact that justifies it: the message-delivery receipt, the payment reference, the recall identifier, the work-order identifier or the invoice line identifier. A row without a resolvable evidence reference is not written. The owner can expand any total to the individual events, and any event to its evidence.

  **FR-7.4.3 — Attribution must not double-count.** An appointment may generate at most one attribution event. Where both a deposit and a confirmed reminder exist, the appointment is credited as `deposit_converted` only. The platform must be able to demonstrate this constraint, because a recovery total that inflates under audit discredits every other number the product reports.

  **FR-7.4.4 — Attribution window.** Each event type carries a configurable window beyond which a subsequent booking is no longer attributed to the platform's intervention. Defaults: 72 hours for `no_show_prevented` and `deposit_converted`, 30 days for `plan_resumed`. Events outside the window are not written rather than written and discounted.

  **FR-7.4.5 — Append-only.** The ledger is subject to the same `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE` treatment as `audit_log` (BR-05, §4.4.3). Corrections are issued as compensating negative rows carrying a reason, never as edits. A clinic disputing a figure must be able to see the correction, not a silently changed total.

  **FR-7.4.6 — Presentation.** The owner dashboard displays the rolling monthly recovered total against the subscription price, and states the break-even explicitly — the number of recovered events at which the subscription is paid for. Conservative per-event values are used by default; the practice may substitute its own realised figures, and the source of each value is disclosed.

### 8.2 Non-Functional Requirements

* **Performance.** Dashboard interactions load within 200 ms on standard 4G or broadband; cold start completes under 1.5 seconds.
* **Availability and reliability.** *(Restated in v2.0.)* **99.9% clinic-perceived availability**, delivered by the offline-first client, with an infrastructure recovery time objective of 20 minutes and a recovery point objective of 5 minutes. Because every operational surface reads and writes IndexedDB first, a server outage does not stop the clinic: reception continues checking patients in, the nurse continues logging vitals, and the queue continues advancing. A single virtual machine cannot deliver 99.9% infrastructure uptime, and a clinical product should not make a claim that fails during a customer's busiest hour.
* **Data sovereignty.** All database instances, backups and media hosted within the territory of India. TLS terminates in Mumbai; DNS is the only third-party service in the request path.
* **Security.** TLS 1.3 in transit; AES-256-GCM at rest; **Argon2id** for password hashing (superseding SHA-256, which is a hash function rather than a password hashing function); strict field-level role-based access control enforced at the database.
* **Usability.** Zero-training design: a new front-desk receptionist registers a walk-in patient within five minutes of onboarding, without a manual.
* **Accessibility.** *(New in v2.0.)* Minimum 48 × 48 px touch targets, rising to 64 × 64 in nurse triage. No information conveyed by colour alone. Script-appropriate line-height for every supported language. `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured.
* **Device and form factor.** *(New in v2.1.)* **Mobile-first is a requirement, not a preference.** Every screen must be complete and usable at 320 px wide, in portrait, on a mid-range Android phone. Specifically:
  * **No horizontal overflow at any supported width.** Content may never extend past the right edge of the viewport. Where a dense artefact genuinely needs more width — a wide data table, a long segmented control — it must sit inside an explicitly scrollable container with a visible affordance, never force the page itself sideways. This is verified automatically by `prototype/check-layout.py` across seven widths from 320 to 1280 px, every view and every seeded tenant, and the check exits non-zero on failure.
  * **No collapsed content.** The converse defect is equally disqualifying: a mis-specified grid can squeeze a screen into a sliver without ever overflowing. The same check asserts that the main content region fills the space available to it.
  * **Touch is the primary input.** Any interaction expressible only through hover, right-click, a keyboard shortcut or HTML5 drag-and-drop must have an equivalent path reachable with one thumb. Primary actions sit within thumb reach, which in practice means the bottom of the screen rather than the top.
  * **Tablet and desktop are progressive enhancements.** They add density — more lanes, real tables, the full dental arch — never capability. No task may be possible on a desktop and impossible on a phone.
  * **Layout is automatic, with a per-device override.** The three layout modes are selected from the viewport by default and may be pinned to Compact, Tablet or Full by the user. The preference is stored on the device and never propagates to other staff. There is exactly one codebase and one set of components; the override changes a single attribute, not a build target.
  * **No iOS zoom-on-focus.** Text inputs render at a minimum of 16 px on touch layouts.
  * **Safe areas and dynamic viewports.** `viewport-fit=cover` with `env(safe-area-inset-*)` respected, and `dvh` rather than `vh`, so a collapsing mobile browser chrome does not clip the last row of the queue.

## 9. Roadmap & Differentiating Innovations

The following capabilities represent the Phase 2 and Phase 3 pipeline. They expand competitive advantage while preserving interface simplicity.

### 9.1 Feature Descriptions

#### 1. Dynamic Token Radar — WhatsApp live queue tracker

A zero-install tracking link sent over WhatsApp 45 minutes before an appointment, showing the patient their live queue position: *"Dr. Sharma is currently consulting Token #3. You are Token #5. Estimated entry 11:20 AM."* If delayed, the patient taps to request a later arrival, and the system automatically swaps their queue position with a patient already waiting, reducing waiting-room crowding.

**The estimator, specified in v2.2.** This feature lives or dies on whether the time it quotes is believed, and a quoted time that is wrong twice is worse than no quote at all. The estimate is therefore not a nominal slot duration:

* Entry time is projected from a **rolling median of actual consultation duration, per practitioner, per session type**, measured as the interval between the `in_service` and `post_service` stage transitions of §4.6.1. The median rather than the mean, because one emergency root canal must not distort the afternoon for everyone behind it.
* A practitioner with insufficient history falls back to the clinic's configured slot length, and the message is hedged accordingly rather than quoting a false precision.
* Queue position is **per practitioner** (FR-2.2), since a clinic-wide token is meaningless to a patient waiting for one specific doctor.
* The estimate is expressed as a range once the projection exceeds twenty minutes out, and the patient is re-notified only when it moves materially — a link that re-messages every five minutes trains patients to mute the clinic.

#### 2. Vernacular post-care messaging sequences

Automated, specialty-specific post-treatment instructions in the patient's chosen language.
*Dentistry:* three hours after an extraction — *"Dr. Sharma's care note: the anaesthesia will wear off shortly. Avoid hot drinks, do not rinse hard, and take your medicine after food."*
*Mental health:* four days after a consultation, an interactive mood check-in whose response updates the therapist's notes for the next session.

#### 3. Vernacular audio informed consent — DPDP and NMC shield

A 15-second audio-recorded informed consent for patients who struggle to read English forms. The tablet plays a clear audio summary of the procedure and risks in Marathi, Telugu, Bengali or Konkani; the patient states their name and speaks their consent. The recording is encrypted, timestamped and linked to the record, creating a legally defensible audit trail.

#### 4. Multilingual ambient voice prescription pad — Speech-to-Rx

An ambient clinical scribe trained on code-mixed Indian medical speech. Clinicians dictate naturally — *"Tab Augmentin 625 BD 5 days, Tab Zerodol-SP TDS after food 3 days, warm saline rinses, call next Thursday"* — and the engine parses this into brand, generic composition, dosage schedule, food instruction and duration, ready for digital signature in under 10 seconds. Fine-tuning of IndicWhisper is funded by Azure GPU credits (Section 5.5).

#### 5. Odontogram-linked treatment acceptance visual explainers

Marking a tooth for a crown or bridge triggers an **Explain to patient** action that sends a 20-second animated explainer to the patient's WhatsApp in their regional language, illustrating why an untreated root-canal tooth can fracture without a crown.

#### 6. Auto-depletion consumable inventory management

Marking a sitting complete automatically deducts its consumables — rotary endodontic file, anaesthetic carpule, rubber dam sheet, sterilisation pouch. Below a configurable buffer, the clinic manager receives a WhatsApp reorder prompt.

#### 7. One-click cashless OPD claims via NHCX

Reception scans the patient's digital insurance card or ABHA ID to verify real-time OPD coverage against the national exchange. At checkout the system submits a structured FHIR claim bundle directly to the insurer, opening the clinic to corporate-insured patients.

### 10.1 Continuous verification gates  *(new in v2.2)*

Three of the guarantees in this document are not features — they are the reasons a clinic can be asked to put patient data into the product at all. In v2.1 all three were demonstrated once, at a milestone review. A guarantee verified once is a guarantee that regresses quietly in week three, and none of these three fail loudly.

They become **CI gates**: they run on every pull request, they block merge, and they are written before the code they protect.

| Gate | Asserts | Fails if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tenant isolation** (FR-1.1.2, §4.4.1) | Two tenants interleaved across a pool of size 1 never observe each other's rows; a request with no tenant context reads zero rows | Any cross-tenant row is returned, or an absent context reads the previous tenant's data |
| **BR-02 under concurrency** (§4.4.2) | N simultaneous booking attempts on one chair and overlapping time range produce exactly one appointment | Two appointments exist, or zero do |
| **Vault non-recoverability** (FR-5.2, §4.4.4) | Note plaintext is unrecoverable from a full database dump plus all server-held secrets, without the patient's PIN | Any plaintext or any single-factor decryption path is reachable |
| **Layout integrity** (§8.2, added in v2.1) | Every view, every tenant, seven widths: no horizontal overflow and no collapsed content | `prototype/check-layout.py` exits non-zero |

The fourth is listed because it is the precedent. It was added in v2.1 after a grid defect collapsed the phone layout to roughly 100 px while producing no horizontal overflow at all — invisible to manual review and invisible to an overflow-only check. The general lesson, and the reason for this section, is that the properties a product sells are exactly the properties that need mechanical verification, because they are the ones everybody assumes are still true.

A note on the isolation gate specifically: it must exercise the real policies against a real Postgres instance, not a mock. An isolation test that does not execute `CREATE POLICY` is testing the test.

## 12. Strategic Conclusion & Execution Mandate

Generic scheduling tools fail in the Indian healthcare market because of low willingness to pay, commoditized alternatives, and an operational mismatch with high-velocity clinics.

PractixOS establishes an enduring position by executing as a **verticalized Practice Operating System**. By integrating deeply into Indian operational realities — zero-app WhatsApp workflows, Indic languages, operatory chair and visiting consultant allocation, PIN-encrypted mental health documentation, and ABDM-conformant interoperability — the platform becomes an indispensable practice engine rather than an easily discarded administrative cost. Its commercial defensibility rests on the Recovered Revenue ledger (§3.3, FR-7.4): a practice that can see, every month, the rupees the platform recovered from its own leakage does not experience the subscription as a cost.

Version 2.0 adds the discipline that makes this survivable as a business. The architecture is entirely free and open source, running at ₹0 per month at pilot scale on infrastructure inside India, with a stated migration path at each stage of growth. The privacy guarantees are enforced by the database rather than asserted by the application. The availability claim is one the product can actually keep. And the design system gives the product a form that matches its thesis: built for Indian clinics, by people who looked closely at how they work.

The remaining risk is execution speed on the two items with external lead times — **ABDM certification and WhatsApp template approval**. Both start in week one.
